Student Account Compromise — Phishing Incident Investigation

Project Overview

This project presents a simulated cybersecurity investigation involving a suspected phishing attack that resulted in possible unauthorized access to a student's account.

The investigation focused on identifying the likely attack technique, analyzing warning signs and available evidence, reconstructing the incident timeline, recommending appropriate incident-response actions, and identifying preventive security controls.

Role

Cybersecurity Analyst

Objectives

- Identify the likely attack technique.
- Analyze phishing and social-engineering indicators.
- Reconstruct the incident timeline.
- Evaluate available evidence and confidence levels.
- Recommend appropriate incident-response actions.
- Identify preventive security controls.

Investigation Summary

The incident began when a student received an urgent email claiming that their mailbox would be suspended unless they verified their account immediately.

The student followed the link provided in the email and entered their credentials on the linked website. Later that evening, the student received an unexpected sign-in notification. On the following Monday, two members reported receiving unusual messages from the student's account.

Based on the available evidence, phishing using social engineering was assessed as the most likely attack.

Key Warning Signs

- Urgent account-verification request
- Threat of mailbox suspension
- Request for account credentials
- Unexpected sign-in notification
- Unusual messages sent from the account

Incident Timeline

1. Phishing email received — An urgent account-verification message was received.
2. Credentials entered — The student entered account credentials on the linked website.
3. Unexpected sign-in — An unexpected sign-in notification was received.
4. Unusual account activity — Members reported unusual messages from the account.

Evidence & Findings

Evidence| Finding| Confidence
Urgent account-verification email| Possible phishing attempt using urgency and fear| Medium
Credentials entered on linked website| Possible credential exposure point| Medium
Unexpected sign-in notification| Strong indicator of possible unauthorized access| High
Unusual messages from the account| Possible unauthorized use of the account| High

The available evidence does not independently confirm who obtained the credentials, whether the website was malicious, or exactly what information was accessed.

Incident Response

Recommended response actions included:

- Change the affected account password.
- Review and revoke suspicious active sessions or devices.
- Preserve relevant evidence.
- Review account security and recovery settings.
- Notify affected users.
- Escalate the incident to the appropriate IT or security team.
- Review available authentication and account activity logs.

Preventive Security Controls

1. Multi-Factor Authentication

MFA provides an additional layer of protection if account credentials are exposed.

2. Cybersecurity Awareness Training

Security awareness training can help users recognize phishing and social-engineering techniques and understand how to report suspicious activity.

3. Least Privilege and Access Control

Limiting users to the access required for their roles can reduce the potential impact of a compromised account.

Key Takeaways

This investigation demonstrated the importance of:

- Evidence-based analysis
- Phishing identification
- Social-engineering awareness
- Incident timeline reconstruction
- Incident response
- Risk reduction
- Distinguishing confirmed observations from assumptions

A strong password alone does not guarantee account security. Layered controls such as MFA, security awareness, and least privilege can help reduce both the likelihood and impact of account compromise.
